What is counted & computation bounds

A step is one application of s(n), the sum of the proper positive divisors. Count through the transition to 0 or the first repeated value. Thus 1 → 0 takes 1 step; a prime → 1 → 0 takes 2; 6 → 6 takes 1; and 220 → 284 → 220 takes 2. Zero has no outgoing step: s(0) is undefined. The axis measures steps, not term values or the number of displayed terms.

Blue points have exact termination times; amber points have exact first-repeat times. Both are clipped at 100 on this logarithmic chart. Seed 138 takes 178 steps to 0 (177 to 1), so it appears at 100+ while its card retains 178. A perfect number or cycle is a resolved outcome, not an escaping orbit.

Hollow upward markers are unresolved computations. If k steps have been checked without termination or repetition, resolution requires at least k + 1 steps. A dashed range starts at that lower bound, when below 100, and extends to the top with an upward arrow. The true length could be finite or infinite; no point is assigned infinity. In particular, the factorization budget stops this dataset’s run from 276 after 79 verified steps: the marker certifies at least 80 steps, not a measured 100-step orbit.

Python’s exact integer arithmetic computes all starts 1–1,000, with at most 256 transitions per start, a 10³⁰ inclusive source-value ceiling, and 2,000,000 trial-factorization work units per new source. Known exact edges are reused across starts. A work-limit failure remains unresolved; no probable-prime result is accepted. Source ceilings and step caps are independent of the 100-step display cap. The browser only plots the precomputed data; changing the range or selecting a point does not launch a calculation.

The download includes every starting record, computation bounds, and the verified edges with proven prime factors. Integer values are decimal strings. Reproduce it with python3 scripts/build_orbit_lengths.py; independent tests check the certificates, paths, and counts. This finite chart does not establish that most integers terminate, or prove a divergent orbit. Different seeds can share the same tail.
