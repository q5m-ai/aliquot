Search options & limits

Inclusive source bound, up to 1,000,000 per sieve. A completed table is reused between steps. A partial search may miss predecessors above its bound.

Test a predecessor outside the scan

Enter a positive integer m (up to 1,000 digits). Only an exact match s(m) = current value becomes selectable. Factorization has a 2,000,000-work-unit budget; exhaustion is unresolved, never a rejection or a probable-prime shortcut.

How to read the graph

The x-axis is the forward step k, with the original target at 0. Going backward adds negative steps to the left. The y-axis is the numeric value (linear or logarithmic). The graph automatically fits the displayed chain and all found predecessors after every choice. Predecessors at the leftmost step are alternatives, not consecutive terms; only your chosen chain is connected. Hovering a candidate previews its forward edge.

All found predecessors are plotted, without pagination. Close values can overlap on screen: hover near them and use ↑/↓ to inspect neighboring values individually. Enter follows the inspected predecessor. Space or Shift-click pins any point for divisor computation without advancing; clicking a chosen point also pins it. On touch screens, tap a predecessor to step back, then tap the chosen point for details.

The latest 180 chosen points are displayed, matching Explore’s rolling window. There is no preset backward-depth limit; the full chosen chain stays in this tab and downloads as exact decimal strings. Reloading starts over. Plot coordinates are approximate, never used in sequence arithmetic.

For n > 1, any predecessor m is composite and has a proper divisor d ≥ √m. Thus n = s(m) ≥ 1 + d and m ≤ (n − 1)². A search covering this ceiling is complete; otherwise, absence is only within the checked range. For target 0 the only predecessor is 1. For target 1 every finite search is partial, because every prime maps to 1. Zero is never a source: s(0) is undefined.

A backward path is not a proof of an unbounded forward orbit. Extending it changes the earliest starting value. Search bounds and browser resources remain finite.
