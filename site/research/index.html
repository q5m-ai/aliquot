Notation s, σ, T, and escape

All number-theoretic variables are integers unless stated otherwise. Iteration stops at zero. Superscript 2 on s or T means composition, not squaring.

n₀ is positive; while nₖ > 0, nₖ₊₁ = s(nₖ) is the sum of its proper positive divisors.
σ(n) = n + s(n); T(u) = 2σ(u) − u.
ℙ = the primes; P(n) = the prime divisors of n; P(1) = ∅.
E means an infinite positive orbit with no repeated terms.

Deficient: s(n) < n. Abundant: s(n) > n. An escaping orbit need not be monotone.

  1. 22 Oct 2026Note 07 · Proposition O
    Finite recovery01

    A decline can precede any finite gain.

    For every fixed deficient core D > 1 and every real G > 1, infinitely many primes p coprime to D give:

    n = Dp, s(n) < n, and s(s(n)) > Gn.

    Finite, not perpetual. The entry conditions do not renew. This example later reaches zero.

    Recovery into a perfect-core class

    A refinement lands above any prescribed gain in a proper multiple r of perfect 28, guaranteeing one further growing step:

    28 divides r and r > 28, so s(r) > r.

    The recovered term is not itself perfect, and is outside this construction’s entry family.

  2. 22 Oct 2026Note 07 · Propositions P–Q
    Template obstruction02

    Periodic prime-cofactor schedules cannot escape.

    Fix cores D₀, …, DL−1 > 1 in a repeating schedule. At every source, the cofactor must be prime and coprime to its core.

    nⱼL₊ᵢ = DᵢpⱼL₊ᵢ for 0 ≤ i < L, with prime pⱼL₊ᵢ coprime to Dᵢ.
    An infinite positive tail of this form satisfies nₖ₊L = nₖ.

    Deficient steps are allowed. Composite cofactors and nonperiodic schedules are not excluded by this result.

    Nonperiodic switching · Proposition Q

    With finitely many fixed cores Dᵢ ≥ 2 and prime, coprime cofactors at every source, an escaping itinerary requires an expansive cycle C in its recurrent transition graph:

    Escape requires a recurrent-graph cycle C whose source-core ratios s(Dᵢ)/Dᵢ multiply to more than 1.

    Necessary, not sufficient. The cycle need not occur consecutively or be repeatable by the actual orbit.

  3. 22 Oct 2026Note 06 · Propositions L–N
    Exact identity03

    Composite factors do not update independently.

    For T(u) = 2σ(u) − u and coprime v, w > 1, the correction is exact:

    T(T(vw)) = T(v)T(w) + 2[s(T(vw)) − s(v)s(w)].

    The guard matters. s(6u) = 6T(u) only while gcd(u, 6) = 1 at each source. The correction does not guarantee renewal.

    A conditional supplier handoff
    29274 → 43302 → 55770. Their cofactors follow 7·17·41 → 7·1031 → 13²·5·11 under T.

    A guarded formula replaces suppliers with composite output blocks. The output does not regenerate the input factorization or the next prime-bridge condition.

  4. 22 Oct 2026Note 07 · Supporting constraint
    Necessary condition04

    Large primes have a quantified cost.

    Write n = dr, where d contains the complete prime-power factors with primes at most B ≥ 2. Let t count the distinct primes in r, all greater than B. For real 0 < ε < 2:

    If σ(d)/d ≤ 2 − ε and n is abundant, then (2 − ε)(1 + 1/B)ᵗ > 2.

    A fixed deficiency margin is essential. Perfect or abundant small-prime parts avoid this hypothesis. Prime-support escape does not supply it.

  5. 21 Oct 2026Note 05 · Proposition K
    S-unit application05

    An escaping orbit cannot keep a fixed prime set.

    P(n) is the set of prime divisors of n, with P(1) = ∅. Only finitely many adjacent aliquot pairs use a prescribed finite set of primes alone.

    For each finite prime set S, only finitely many n > 1 have all prime factors of both n and s(n) in S.
    If E holds, infinitely many distinct primes occur. Whether any such seed exists remains open.

    Conditional on escape. This does not force a new prime at every step, or a decrease when large primes appear.

    From source values to orbit indices
    If E holds, then for each finite prime set S, only finitely many adjacent orbit pairs are supported on S.

    Nonrepetition is essential: finitely many source values can otherwise recur at infinitely many indices.

The missing step

Growth is not yet renewal.

Construction needs arithmetic entry conditions that reproduce themselves indefinitely. Contradiction needs a bound or decreasing quantity covering every orbit. Neither is established here.
